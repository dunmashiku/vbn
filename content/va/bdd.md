## Who can file a BDD claim

Benefits Delivery at Discharge (BDD) lets full-time active duty service members, including National Guard, Reserve and Coast Guard members on full-time active duty, file a VA disability claim before they leave the military. You need a known separation date and must have between 180 and 90 days left on active duty when you file.

You must be available for VA exams during the 45 days after you file. Filing early means VA can review the claim while you are still serving, so a decision can come sooner after separation.

## What to include

A BDD claim must include the Separation Health Assessment Part A Self-Assessment form (https://www.benefits.va.gov/compensation/docs/SHA_DBQ_Part_A_Self-Assessment.pdf). If you file online, VA requests your service treatment records for you. If you file another way, you need to send copies of your service treatment records yourself.

All parts of the VA and Department of Defense separation exam process must be finished before you are released from the military.

## Timing rules and exceptions

You can add conditions to a BDD claim until 90 days before your expected discharge. Conditions added later may not be processed until after you separate.

If you have fewer than 90 days left, BDD is not available, but you can still file a standard claim (https://www.va.gov/disability/how-to-file-claim/evidence-needed/standard-claims).

Some situations can't use BDD even with more than 90 days left: being in case management for a serious injury or illness, a terminal illness, waiting for discharge while being treated at a VA or military treatment facility, awaiting a character of discharge determination, or not being able to attend VA exams in the 45-day period.

## How to file and get help

If you are stationed in the U.S., file online or with a VA-accredited representative (https://www.va.gov/get-help-from-accredited-representative). Your Transition Assistance Office (ACAP for the Army) can help you book a VA benefits briefing. If you are stationed overseas, you can file online, with a representative, or at a BDD office.

Service members who are wounded, ill or injured and can't perform their duties are usually referred to the Integrated Disability Evaluation System (IDES) instead, which can give a proposed VA rating before discharge.
