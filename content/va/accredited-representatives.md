## Who can help with a VA claim

VA's Office of General Counsel accredits three kinds of representatives: Veterans Service Organization (VSO) representatives, attorneys and claims agents. Only accredited people may help you prepare, present or prosecute a VA claim. You can search for one with VA's tool (https://www.va.gov/get-help-from-accredited-representative/find-rep/).

## Help from a VSO is always free

Representatives from VA-recognized Veterans Service Organizations never charge for their help. You usually appoint the organization as a whole, so you can work with different representatives there without new paperwork. VA lists recognized VSOs at https://www.va.gov/ogc/apps/accreditation/accredvso.asp. State and county veterans offices also offer free help.

## When fees are allowed

Accredited attorneys and claims agents may charge fees only after VA has decided your initial claim, and only if VA has received a fee agreement signed by you and them, and VA Form 21-22a appointing them. Filing an initial claim never requires payment.

## Watch out for claim sharks

Some companies that are not accredited charge veterans large fees, sometimes a share of future benefits, to help with claims. That is not allowed for initial claims. You can file a complaint with VA's Office of General Counsel about someone helping with claims without accreditation (https://www.va.gov/OGC/docs/Accred/HowtoFileaComplaint.pdf).

## Appointing and changing a representative

You appoint a VSO with VA Form 21-22 and an attorney or claims agent with VA Form 21-22a. You can end the arrangement at any time, for example by calling VA at 800-827-1000 (TTY 711).
