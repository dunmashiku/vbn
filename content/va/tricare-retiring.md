## Enroll within 90 days

Retiring from active duty is a TRICARE Qualifying Life Event. You have 90 days after your retirement date to enroll in a TRICARE health plan. To keep TRICARE Prime or TRICARE Select with no break in coverage, you must re-enroll within those 90 days.

If you miss the 90 days, you can still ask for retroactive enrollment up to 12 months from your retirement date. Coverage then starts on your retirement date, but claims aren't paid until enrollment is complete, and you owe enrollment fees back to that date. After 12 months, you can only enroll during TRICARE Open Season or after another qualifying life event. If you never enroll, you lose TRICARE coverage.

## Plan options for retirees

Retiree plan options include TRICARE Prime (in Prime Service Areas), TRICARE Select, the US Family Health Plan and TRICARE Prime Demo (in specific U.S. locations), TRICARE For Life (requires Medicare Part A and Part B) and TRICARE Select Overseas.

## What changes when you retire

Retirees pay annual enrollment fees for Prime and Select, plus copays and cost-shares that are higher than on active duty. Costs change every year. If you are eligible for Medicare, you must have Medicare Part B to keep TRICARE.

Some services are no longer covered after retirement, including hearing aids (except for certain children), chiropractic care and routine eye exams outside Prime. Dental and vision coverage can be bought through FEDVIP (https://www.benefeds.gov/); vision requires being enrolled in a TRICARE health plan.

## Before your last day

Schedule your Separation History and Physical Examination (SHPE) 90 to 180 days before you retire or start terminal leave (https://www.health.mil/SHPE). Terminal leave is not a qualifying life event, and TRICARE Prime continues during it.

TRICARE is separate from VA health care. Many retirees also apply for VA health care, which is a different program with its own eligibility.
