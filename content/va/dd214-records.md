## Why the DD-214 matters

Your DD-214 (Certificate of Release or Discharge from Active Duty) is the main proof of your service. Almost every VA and state veteran benefit asks for it. It shows your character of discharge, dates of service, duty assignments, medals and decorations, and military education.

If you apply for VA benefits, VA requests your DD-214 itself when your application arrives, so you don't need to request it separately just for VA.

## Review it before you sign

Check your DD-214 worksheet carefully before you separate: dates, awards, military education, character of service and separation codes. Errors are much easier to fix while you are still serving. Discharge upgrades and record corrections after separation go through your branch's review boards (https://www.va.gov/discharge-upgrade-instructions).

## How to get a copy

Online: request records from the National Archives through eVetRecs (https://www.archives.gov/). Choose "Make a new request" and sign in with an identity-verified ID.me account.

Through the Department of Defense: milConnect (https://milconnect.dmdc.osd.mil/milconnect/) has DD-214s for people discharged after these dates: Air Force, October 1, 2004; Army, October 1, 2002; Marine Corps, January 1, 1999; Navy, January 1, 1995.

By mail or fax: use Standard Form 180, Request Pertaining to Military Records (https://www.va.gov/forms/sf180). The chart on the form's last page tells you where to send it based on your branch, separation date and the records you need.

## Family members

Next of kin of a deceased veteran (a surviving spouse who hasn't remarried, a parent, child or sibling) can also request records, with proof of death such as a death certificate or a funeral home letter.
