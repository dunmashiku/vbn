## States offer their own benefits

Every state and most territories have a veterans affairs office that runs benefits separate from VA. Common state benefits include property tax relief, income tax breaks on military retired pay, tuition help at state schools, license and vehicle fee waivers, state veterans homes and cemeteries, and veteran hiring preference.

State veterans offices also employ veterans service officers who can help with VA claims and state benefits for free.

## Finding your state office

VA lists state veterans affairs offices in its external resources directory (https://discover.va.gov/external-resources/?_resource_type=state-veterans-affairs-office). You can filter by state to get the agency's website, mailing address and phone number. The National Association of State Directors of Veterans Affairs also links to every state office (http://nasdva.us/).

## Example: Maryland

Maryland's office is the Maryland Department of Veterans and Military Families (https://veterans.maryland.gov/), 16 Francis Street, 4th floor, Annapolis, MD 21401, phone 410-260-3838 (Maryland Relay 711). Its Benefits and Services section covers how to apply for benefits, health care, education, burial, housing, tax exemptions, motor vehicle services, benefits for service-connected veterans, financial support and a Veterans Services Specialist program. Landing Zone Maryland helps veterans and military families who are new to the state.

Rules and amounts differ by state and change often, so confirm details with your state office.
