## Who decides and when

The Department of Defense, not VA, decides whether you can transfer Post-9/11 GI Bill benefits to a spouse or children. You must request the transfer while you are still serving on active duty or in the Selected Reserve. VA cannot accept a transfer request, and you can't start one after you separate.

## Eligibility

All of these must be true: you have at least 6 years of service on the date the transfer is approved, you agree to serve 4 more years, and the family member is enrolled in DEERS. If you received a Purple Heart, the service requirement doesn't apply, but you still must request the transfer while on active duty.

You can transfer up to 36 months of your remaining benefits in total.

## How to request a transfer

Submit a Transfer of Education Benefits (TEB) request through milConnect (https://milconnect.dmdc.osd.mil/milconnect/). Your branch's education office can help. You can change or cancel transfers that haven't been used yet through milConnect.

## When family members can use benefits

A spouse can use transferred benefits right away. If you separated on or after January 1, 2013, there is no time limit for a spouse. A child can start using benefits only after you have at least 10 years of service, and must have a high school diploma or be at least 18, and be under 26.

Family members apply themselves, online with their own account or with VA Form 22-1990e (https://www.va.gov/forms/22-1990e).

## If you leave early

If you don't finish the extra service, your family may lose eligibility and you may owe VA for benefits already paid, unless you separated for reasons such as a medical condition, hardship discharge, force reduction or injury.
